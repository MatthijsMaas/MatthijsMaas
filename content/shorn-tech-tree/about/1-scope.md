---
title: "Scope of analysis"
---
The focus of this project is to chart the growth--and blunting--of the **global technological capability [wavefront](https://www.nellwatson.com/blog/technological-wavefront)**.

As such, it is primarily concerned with identifying the 'high water mark' (or, to mix metaphors, the most mature tech tree branch) for a particular technological path.

This means I focus on:

- **pruned branches**: cases where an incipient pursued technology or research program would (it was perceived) have provided someone with a *new* capability or affordance (whether in a qualitative sense, or in some quantitative sense, as setting the [next benchmark](https://aiimpacts.org/cases-of-discontinuous-technological-progress/) on some particular metric of performance), but was nonetheless abandoned (e.g. performative proposals, crushed seeds);
- **ebbed innovations**: cases where a technology was prototyped or even deployed, but then mostly abandoned or dismantled, in a way where it resulted in a local (and potentially persisting) loss of a particular capability or affordance.

This aims to **exclude cases of straightforward 'obsolescence'**: i.e. I'm not interested in technologies whose cancellation or abandonment can primarily or exclusively be explained by the appearance of obvious 'replacement technologies' (i.e. those that "[not merely improve earlier devices but supersedes them entirely](https://www.jstor.org/stable/43521391)").
