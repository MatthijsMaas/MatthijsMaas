---
title: "How to read these"
---
This page contains a long list of technologies; reading from the top may be a bit overwhelming or deadening, so I recommend you just pick a category and sub-category (for instance by clicking a row or column in the table) and skim through it for any ones that catch your eye.

Cases are not presented strongly chronologically, but they are categorized along 'type of abandonment (high water mark)', and, for each, the type or domain of technology.

**Things to keep in mind when reading this list.** I recommend you try and read this list imagining that you don't already know the eventual outcome:

- try and imagine living at the time and hearing that a particular technology or project had just received a major government grant or private investment (as indeed many did); and then see how you feel about the scientific basis, technological feasibility, and economic or military utility or competitiveness of each proposal.
- try and imagine if someone had told you that a particular technology had been developed; that the world had attempted to restrain it, but that international efforts to achieve arms control for the technology had failed. What ('[virtual historiography](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/hith.12247)') stories might you have come up with to explain why the technology was irrevocably critical and restraint would always have failed?

For any abandoned technology where an account is given of the (frequently cited) reasons for its abandonment, consider for a moment (1) are those the real reasons or the whole story? (2) if they were the real (organizational) reason for cancellation, were they good assessments of project barriers, i.e. ones that really could not have been overcome?

**What does this mean?** There are many ways that you can read these lists: as a sign that technology is more fragile than we think, or as evidence that many technological dreams remain resilient and eventually spring anew; as a sign of all the things that we managed to lock away; or all the ones we could still build. As a sign of the breadth of our dreams, or of the foolishness of our hype. As inspiration for attempts to slow technologies and/or steer the directions of fields; as societal shopping list for what projects should be revived next, or as inspiration for your next start-up.
