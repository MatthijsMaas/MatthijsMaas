---
title: "Quotes on ‘failed’ technologies"
---
As [Howard Jones](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/895234/pdf) has argued,

> If success be translated into historical opportunism, is failure conditioned merely by arriving at the right spot but at the wrong time? ... The history of technology is also a way of understanding man, but I suggest that the "failures" may be more illuminating than the "successes." ... Above all, the historian of technology must avoid the vulgar error of believing that nothing succeeds like success.

The challenge is epistemic. Large parts of the tech tree may have been pruned at an early stage--in a way that may have been premature; and in a way that is mostly invisible to us. Where some records of their existence are preserved, we may easily misinterpret the causes of their abandonment, in ways that make us unjustifiably confident that those branches were inherently barren (or inferior) anyway. As the historian of technology [Thomas Misa](https://theconvivialsociety.substack.com/p/manufactured-inevitability-and-the) notes:

> … [W]e lack a full picture of the technological alternatives that once existed as well as knowledge and understanding of the decision-making processes that winnowed them down. We see only the results and assume, understandably but in error, that there was no other path to the present. Yet it is a truism that the victors write the history, in technology as in war, and the technological 'paths not taken' are often suppressed or ignored.
