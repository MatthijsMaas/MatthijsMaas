---
title: "Roadmap and definitions of categories"
---
The categories, arranged as the tree in the figure, from the summit down: the lower a category, the earlier its technologies were cut.

**The narrow summit** (concentrated successes):

- **Steep plateaus** – Achieved by only one or a few actors, leaving global dependence on them (EUV lithography, nuclear icebreakers, jet-engine etc.).

**The high branches** (limit cases: the technology exists, but a buyer or a country failed to get it or lost it):

- **Procurement and deployment bungles** – Cancelled procurements and failed rollouts where a replacement still exists.
- **Locally atrophied branches** – i.e. situations where a country used to be able to build something, but no longer can (while other actors still can and do).

**The branches** (deployed, then lost, stunted or delayed):

- **Withered branches** – i.e. technologies that were adopted, then mostly or completely phased out without being clearly outperformed (on all relevant functions or dimensions).
- **Inferior leaps** – Competitions where a worse technology won, temporarily or persistently.
- **Underpowered dreams** – i.e. technologies that arrived and persisted, but whose societal impacts proved more marginal than expected.
- **Protracted adoption** – i.e. technologies that were initially opposed, but eventually succeeded (a control group of near-misses).
- **False first dawns** – i.e. technologies that failed initially, then succeeded decades later under new circumstances.

**The trunk** (researched or prototyped, never deployed):

- **Forlorn prototypes** – i.e. operational prototypes built and tested, then scrapped.
- **Development hell** – i.e. technologies subject to decades of sustained attempts (including, potentially, contemporary revivals), but where all have failed to date.
- **Abandoned development** – i.e. projects subject to some research and development efforts, at times with significant sunk costs, before being cancelled.
- **Dead ends and science fictions** – i.e. technologies that have been fervently and often repeatedly pursued, but never borne fruit; likely because they are scientifically implausible.

**The roots** (never pursued beyond the proposal):

- **Crushed seeds** – i.e. proposed or theorised ideas but never seriously pursued (beyond minimal design studies), despite apparent feasibility.
- **Performative proposals** – i.e. vaporware, PR stunts, sales or recruitment flyers, sacrificial budget items.

**The soil** (field-wide losses):

- **Lost 'dark matter' innovations** – i.e. broad classes of innovation suppression by external factors, even though particular technological losses may be hard to discern.
